## LinkedIn Content Strategy

LinkedIn content will focus on useful professional insights and proof of practical work rather than daily course-completion updates.

### Content Sources

Future LinkedIn content can come from:

1. Learning insights
2. Practical exercises
3. Simulation projects
4. Real business projects
5. Case studies
6. Data analysis
7. Growth experiments
8. CRM and automation workflows
9. AI-assisted workflows
10. Measurable business results

### Content Framework

A useful post can follow:

**Problem → Insight → Example → Action → Learning**

For example:

**Problem:** What growth problem was being considered?

**Insight:** What did I learn or discover?

**Example:** How did I apply the idea?

**Action:** What did I test or build?

**Learning:** What changed my understanding?

### Content Categories

#### 1. Learning Insight

Example:

"3 things I learned about customer acquisition while studying growth funnels."

#### 2. Practical Build

Example:

"I built a simple funnel analysis framework and learned why measuring only traffic can be misleading."

#### 3. Case Study

Example:

"How I analyzed a customer acquisition problem and identified potential conversion opportunities."

#### 4. Experiment

Example:

"I tested a new follow-up approach and tracked its effect on lead conversion."

#### 5. Real Business Result

Example:

"After implementing a new growth workflow, here is what changed."

Real results must always be supported by actual data.

### Posting Frequency

I do not need to post every day.

A strong post should be published when there is something genuinely useful, interesting, practical, or evidence-based to share.

The goal is to build professional credibility, not maintain a daily course diary.

### Day Completion Rule

Completing a learning day does not automatically require a LinkedIn post.

Daily work belongs primarily in:

**Daily Log → Evidence → GitHub → Master Tracker**

Only selected insights or significant work should become LinkedIn content.
